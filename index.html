MODULE 01 — FUNDAMENTALS

What is an LVDT?

A Linear Variable Differential Transformer (LVDT) is an electromechanical transducer that converts the linear (straight-line) mechanical displacement of a moving object into a corresponding electrical signal. It is one of the most widely used position/displacement sensors in precision engineering, robotics, and industrial automation.

Key insight: Unlike potentiometers or encoders, the LVDT has absolutely no mechanical contact between the moving part (core) and the sensing coils. This makes it virtually frictionless, giving it an infinite theoretical resolution and extremely long service life — ideal for robotics.
🎯

What it Measures

Linear displacement (position) — how far an object has moved along a straight line — with sub-micron resolution in precision models.

⚡

How it Works

Mutual inductance between a primary coil and two secondary coils changes as a ferromagnetic core moves. The differential output voltage indicates position.

♾️

Infinite Resolution

Since there is no physical contact, there is no quantization from teeth or slots. Resolution is limited only by the signal conditioning electronics.

🤖

Why Robotics Loves It

Frictionless, no electrical contact, immune to vibration and harsh environments, and delivers absolute position output — perfect for end-effectors, actuators, and joints.

📡

Output Type

AC voltage output proportional to displacement. Signal conditioning circuits convert this to ±10 V DC or 4–20 mA for PLC/microcontroller interfacing.

🌡️

Rugged & Reliable

Operates from −55°C to +150°C. Works in high-vibration, high-pressure, corrosive, and vacuum environments where other sensors fail.

MODULE 02 — CONSTRUCTION

Inside an LVDT: Parts & Materials

An LVDT is elegantly simple. It has two main mechanical assemblies: the cylindrical housing containing the coil assembly, and the free-moving ferromagnetic core.

  • P-1
    Primary Coil (P)The center coil, excited by an AC excitation voltage (typically 1–10 kHz, 1–10V RMS). It generates the magnetic flux that links to both secondary coils. Wound on the central bobbin section.
  • S-1
    Secondary Coil 1 (S₁)Wound to the left of the primary. When the core moves left, more flux links through S₁, increasing its induced EMF. Connected in series-opposition with S₂.
  • S-2
    Secondary Coil 2 (S₂)Wound to the right of the primary, wound in the opposite direction to S₁. Its EMF is subtracted from S₁'s EMF to produce the differential output.
  • C-1
    Ferromagnetic CoreMade of high-permeability nickel-iron alloy (or soft iron). Slides freely inside the coil bore. Physically attached to the object being measured. This is the only moving part.
  • H-1
    Housing / FormerStainless steel or aluminium outer shell. The coil winding bobbin is typically made of glass-reinforced plastic (GRP) or ceramic for dimensional stability.
  • SC
    Signal Conditioning ElectronicsOscillator (generates AC excitation), demodulator (converts AC output to DC), and amplifier/filter. Often integrated inside modern LVDT packages.

Null (Centre) Position

When the core is exactly at the mechanical centre (between S₁ and S₂), it couples equally with both secondary coils. The mutual inductance M₁ = M₂.

Both secondaries induce the same magnitude of EMF. Since S₁ and S₂ are wound and connected in opposition, their voltages cancel exactly.

V_out = V_S1 − V_S2 = 0 V

This is the electrical zero or null point. The null position is extremely stable and reproducible — better than ±0.1% in high-quality LVDTs.

Zero error: Any small residual voltage at null position is called "null voltage" or "residual voltage." It's typically 0.1–1% of full-scale output.

Core Displaced Left (Negative)

As the core moves toward S₁, the magnetic flux linking through S₁ increases (mutual inductance M₁ rises). Simultaneously, flux through S₂ decreases (mutual inductance M₂ falls).

By Faraday's law: more flux linkage = more induced EMF. So V_S1 > V_S2.

The differential output: V_out = V_S1 − V_S2 > 0 V

The magnitude of V_out is proportional to how far the core has moved from null. The phase of V_out is the same as the excitation signal.

⚠ The output is still AC. To know direction, you must demodulate it using a phase-sensitive detector (PSD), which produces a signed DC output.

Core Displaced Right (Positive)

Now the core moves toward S₂. Flux through S₂ increases, flux through S₁ decreases. So V_S2 > V_S1.

The differential output: V_out = V_S1 − V_S2 < 0 V

Magnitude is still proportional to displacement, but the polarity is reversed. The AC output is now 180° out of phase with the excitation signal — the demodulator uses this phase reversal to determine direction.

Phase reversal trick: At null, output phase shifts 180°. This is how the electronics determine which side of null the core is on.

Reading the Output

The raw output of an LVDT is an AC signal. To use it with a PLC or microcontroller, two steps are needed:

1. Demodulation: A phase-sensitive demodulator (PSD or synchronous detector) converts the AC differential output to a DC signal. It compares the output phase to the excitation and assigns a sign (+/−).

2. Filtering: A low-pass filter removes ripple from the demodulated signal, leaving a clean DC voltage proportional to position.

Modern DC-LVDT or DC-DC LVDT modules integrate the oscillator and demodulator internally. You just supply 24V DC and get ±10V DC or 4–20mA out — plug directly into a PLC analog input.

MODULE 05 — MATHEMATICS

The Math Behind LVDT

Faraday's Law — Induced EMF

The induced voltage in any coil is given by Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction:

e = −N × (dΦ/dt) = −M × (dI_p/dt)
e = induced EMF (V) N = number of turns Φ = magnetic flux (Wb) M = mutual inductance (H) I_p = primary current (A)

Mutual Inductance vs. Position

The mutual inductance between the primary and each secondary changes linearly with core displacement (in the linear range):

M₁ = M₀ + K·x     M₂ = M₀ − K·x
M₀ = mutual inductance at null K = sensitivity constant (H/m) x = displacement from null (m)

Output Voltage Equations

If the excitation is V_p = V_exc · sin(ωt), then the secondary voltages and differential output are:

V_S1 = jωM₁I_p    V_S2 = jωM₂I_p

V_out = V_S1 − V_S2 = jω(M₁ − M₂)I_p = jω(2Kx)I_p
|V_out| = 2ωKI_p · x = S · x
S = sensitivity (V/mm) ω = 2πf = angular frequency I_p = primary current amplitude
Key result: V_out is directly proportional to displacement x. This is the fundamental operating principle. Sensitivity S can be increased by raising excitation frequency ω or amplitude, or by optimising the geometry (K). In practice, most signal conditioners hold the demodulated output referenced only to position and amplitude, so students should expect the RMS reading (not the carrier's cycle count) to track those two — see the note in Module 03.

MODULE 06 — SIGNAL CONDITIONING

Signal Conditioning Chain

Raw LVDT output must be processed before connecting to a PLC or controller. Here's the complete signal conditioning chain:

〰️

1. Oscillator

Generates a precise sinusoidal AC excitation signal (frequency: 1–10 kHz). Frequency must be stable — frequency drift causes sensitivity error. Wien-bridge or crystal oscillators are common.

📶

2. Amplifier

Amplifies the weak differential output from the LVDT secondaries. High CMRR (Common Mode Rejection Ratio) instrumentation amplifiers (e.g. INA128) are used to reject noise common to both outputs.

🔄

3. Demodulator (PSD)

A Phase-Sensitive Detector (PSD) or synchronous demodulator multiplies the amplified output by the excitation reference to produce a DC output with correct sign (±). This gives direction information.

📉

4. Low-Pass Filter

Removes the double-frequency ripple (2ω) and high-frequency noise from the demodulated signal, leaving a clean DC output proportional to position. Cutoff frequency = signal BW.

🔌

5. Output Stage

Converts filtered DC to a standard interface: ±10V DC (for servo drives), 4–20 mA (for long-distance PLC links), 0–5V (for ADC input), or RS-485/CAN digital.

🛡️

6. Protection

ESD protection, over-voltage clamping, and short-circuit protection on the output. In industrial environments, galvanic isolation (using transformers or optocouplers) protects the controller.

DC-LVDT module: Modern integrated DC-LVDT signal conditioners (e.g. TE Connectivity LDC series, Measurement Specialties) include all these stages on a single PCB. Input: 24V DC. Output: ±10V or 4–20mA. Just connect, calibrate, and read — ideal for PLC integration in robotics.
MODULE 07 — APPLICATIONS

LVDT in Robotics & Automation

LVDTs are embedded throughout modern robotics and industrial automation systems. Their frictionless operation, high accuracy, and robustness make them irreplaceable in these applications:

🦾
ROBOTICS

Robotic Arm Joint Position

Mounted on linear actuators in robotic joints to provide precise position feedback for closed-loop control. Replaces potentiometers with superior linearity and unlimited life.

🔧
MANUFACTURING

Hydraulic Cylinder Position

Attached to piston rods in hydraulic actuators on CNC machines and presses. Provides servo-quality feedback at pressures up to 350 bar. Core rod is the piston rod itself.

🏭
AUTOMATION

End Effector / Gripper

Mounted in gripper jaws to measure jaw opening and detect part presence/size. Enables force-controlled grasping when combined with a spring-loaded core.

🚗
AUTOMOTIVE

Suspension & Ride Height

In test rigs and active suspension systems, LVDTs measure wheel travel and ride height. Used in EV battery pack assembly robots for precise vertical positioning.

✈️
AEROSPACE

Flight Control Actuators

Control surface position (flaps, ailerons) measured by LVDTs in fly-by-wire systems. MIL-spec LVDTs survive −55°C to +150°C with 50g vibration — no encoder can match this.

🏗️
CIVIL

Structural Monitoring

Embedded in bridges and dams to measure dynamic deflection. Robots used for structural inspection use LVDTs to measure probe contact displacement in crack detection.

💊
MEDICAL

Surgical Robots

In minimally invasive surgical robots, micro-LVDTs (as small as 4mm diameter) measure the position of instrument tips inside the patient for force and position feedback.

🔬
PRECISION

CMM (Metrology)

Coordinate Measuring Machines use arrays of LVDTs in their touch-trigger probes. Resolution below 0.01 μm. LVDTs' infinite resolution makes them ideal for dimensional inspection robots.

MODULE 09 — ROBOTIC SIMULATION

Robotic Arm Contact & Force Sensing

Many robotic grippers use a spring-loaded LVDT probe as an indirect force sensor. As the end-effector advances toward a workpiece, the probe's core is pushed back against an internal bias spring. A soft material indents easily, so most of the motion is absorbed by the material and the core barely moves. A rigid material barely indents, so nearly all the motion compresses the spring — moving the core (and the LVDT reading) quickly. Select a material and slide the arm forward to see it happen.
